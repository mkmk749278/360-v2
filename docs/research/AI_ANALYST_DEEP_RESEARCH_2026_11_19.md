# Make the AI analyst a disciplined executor

The evidence says a next-generation AI analyst will not beat this book by predicting direction better. Every controlled, post-training-cutoff study of LLM traders finds little or no durable alpha from models choosing direction ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v5); [Profit Mirage](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07920v1)), and the owner's own study found no market-state label that predicts next-day alt direction in both halves of 2026. What the evidence does support is narrower. It can apply a small, regime-matched playbook at levels where orders cluster. It can put stops beyond structure and targets at opposing levels. It can refuse trades with no location, enter at a price that survives fees, and leave the right tail alone, because in crypto **a handful of large winners carries the return** ([Springer FMPM 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)). The owner's measured history (about 4,900 closed signals, June–November 2026, supplied with this brief) fits that reading. A **+0.13%/trade gross edge** sits inside a **0.07–0.10% round-trip fee**. Entry drift takes it below zero (+0.14% stamped falls to −0.06% at the observed price). The **top 1% of trades carries ~80% of gross profit**. About 22% of trades give back a median +2.25% favourable excursion at break-even. Prediction is not the binding constraint. Execution, cost, tail capture and exit management are, and those are the problems a pre-planned, limit-order setup changes, in both directions. The analyst's job is to read a pre-computed context, pick from an engine-supplied level menu, and say "no setup" most hours. It has to be judged in a dark, forward, paired test against a fixed-rule arm on the same menu. That test needs **roughly 2,500–3,000 independent trades** to resolve an edge of this size, so the commitment is months, not weeks.

*Labelling convention.* Claims followed by a link are cited evidence, graded in the tables below. Statements prefixed **Inference** are reasoning from that evidence or from the owner's figures, and nobody has measured them. The owner's book statistics come from the brief, not from a public source. Arithmetic on them is shown so it can be checked.

## The book's numbers point at cost, tails and execution, not prediction

Start with the arithmetic, because it settles what the analyst is for. **Inference (owner's figures):** with an average stop of −2.4% and an average TP1 of +3.6%, a pure win/lose book needs a 40% hit rate to break even (2.4 / 6.0). The book wins 30–34%. It is still positive gross only because a block of trades closes near zero (the ~22% break-even scratches) and because a very fat right tail sits outside the TP1 arithmetic. The owner's 95% CI of roughly ±0.08% on ~4,900 trades implies a **per-trade standard deviation near 2.8%** (SE ≈ 0.04%; 0.04 × √4,900 ≈ 2.8). That one number sets the sample sizes in the evaluation section.

The tail concentration matters most. **Inference:** a +0.13% mean over 4,900 trades is about 640 percentage points of gross PnL. If 80% of it comes from the top 49 trades, they average roughly +10% each, and the remaining 99% of the book averages about +0.03% gross, which is **negative after any realistic fee**. So the strategy is the tail, and everything else is cost. That matches the literature exactly. In crypto momentum, one outlier accounts for 37% of the compounded return and the tail exponent sits below 3, so variance is statistically undefined ([Springer FMPM 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)). A BTC 200-day trend system wins only 23% of the time and is carried by its large winners ([BoringEdge](https://boringedge.com/bitcoin-200-day-moving-average-strategy-backtest/)). Crypto is a lottery-like, positively skewed asset class ([MAX Momentum, Reading](https://centaur.reading.ac.uk/99101/1/MAX%20Momentum.R1.pdf)). Two consequences follow. A filter or exit rule judged on win rate will tend to destroy the book while looking like an improvement. And any small-sample verdict can flip sign on one trade.

Costs explain why the edge has looked indistinguishable from zero since October. Every cost-aware source agrees that drag scales with turnover and dominates intraday edges. At just 2 bps one-way, BTC's day/night session edge falls from **4.24 bps to 0.14 bps a day** ([JRFM](https://doi.org/10.3390/jrfm19090692)). Short-holding-period momentum degrades most once costs and liquidations are modelled ([AUT ACFR](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf)). Momentum in larger coins "incurs substantial trading costs" ([IRFA 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1057521924001509)). **Inference:** taker-in, taker-out at 0.05% is 10 bps before slippage, and sub-$1 movers add materially more. Any setup type whose gross expectancy is under roughly 15–20 bps is a fee generator, and today's book average sits in that zone.

The owner's regime split is consistent with the published conditioning. Volatile and trending-up states carry the book, and trending-down is negative. Time-series momentum in crypto is strong and concentrated in bull markets, and it loses in most falling-market cases ([AUT ACFR](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf)). LLM agents are specifically documented as **too conservative in bulls and too aggressive in bears** ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v5)), which is the wrong way round for this book. The negative label study and the positive regime split do not conflict. A label can fail to *forecast* tomorrow's direction and still separate the states in which a given archetype has paid from those in which it has not. **Inference:** regime belongs in the analyst's input as a condition on *which playbook is eligible and how much risk it takes*. It is not a directional forecast.

## Five things have real evidence; most of the trader toolkit does not

The research lanes converge on a short list. The table grades each input against the evidence base. "Strong" means several independent peer-reviewed sources, robust to costs. "Moderate" means peer-reviewed but one sample, gross, or pre-2021. "Weak" means a practitioner study. "Folklore" means widely repeated with no test found.

| Input or check | What the evidence says | Grade | Role for the analyst |
|---|---|---|---|
| Transaction costs vs gross expectancy | Intraday edges collapse at a few bps; short holds degrade most ([JRFM](https://doi.org/10.3390/jrfm19090692); [AUT](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf)) | **Strong** (direction) | Hard floor: refuse a setup whose distance to the first target cannot clear fees plus slippage at a realistic hit rate |
| Fat right tail / skew | One outlier is 37% of momentum returns; trend wins ~23% ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9); [BoringEdge](https://boringedge.com/bitcoin-200-day-moving-average-strategy-backtest/)) | **Moderate–strong** | Never cap the runner by default; judge every rule on total PnL with and without the top trades |
| Passive-fill adverse selection | Crypto limit fill rates of ~2% ([arXiv 2307.04863](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04863)) and 8–25%, with fills skewed toward wrong signals ([Microstructure Lab](https://themicrostructurelab.substack.com/p/execution-simulation-for-hf-directional)); negative post-fill drift ([DeLise](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.16527)) | **Moderate–strong** | Every limit-entry setup must be scored on missed fills too; the entry zone is a trade-off, not a free improvement |
| HTF trend / time-series momentum as a regime filter | Daily trend on BTC earns most of its edge by avoiding bear drawdowns ([Grayscale](https://research.grayscale.com/reports/the-trend-is-your-friend-managing-bitcoins-volatility-with-momentum-signals)); TSMOM is bull-concentrated ([AUT](https://acfr.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/918729/Time_Series_and_Cross_Sectional_Momentum_in_the_Cryptocurrency_Market_with_IA.pdf)) | **Moderate** | Gate: no long continuation setups against a falling daily structure |
| BTC state for alts | Alts behave as leveraged BTC; correlation goes to 1 in stress ([Amberdata](https://blog.amberdata.io/performance-under-fire-2025s-risk-adjusted-reality); [TokenPost/Kaiko](https://tokenpost.com/news/insights/20585)) | **Moderate** | Gate alt longs on BTC regime; read BTC first every hour |
| Momentum in liquid names, reversal in illiquid ones | Large liquid coins show weekly momentum; reversal sits in small, low-volume coins ([Fičura](https://quantitative.cz/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/impact_of_size_and_volume_on_cryptocurrency_momentum_and_reversal.pdf); [Dobrynskaya](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3913263)) | **Moderate** | Continuation longs belong on liquid names; extreme hourly gainers lean toward reversal |
| Levels as locations, via order clustering | Published S/R bounced 60.8%, only ~4–6pp above random levels ([Osler 2000](https://resources.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)); take-profits cluster *at* round numbers, stops just *beyond* ([Osler 2003](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v58y2003i5p1791-1819.html)) | **Moderate** (FX; untested in crypto) | Levels define where to enter and where invalidation is, not whether to trade |
| Stops add value only with autocorrelation | Under a random walk stop rules lower expected return; with momentum they can add 50–100 bp/month ([Kaminski & Lo](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/114876/Lo_When%20Do%20Stop-Loss.pdf)) | **Moderate** | Tight stops and early break-even make sense only in archetypes with measured persistence |
| New-listing and pump decay | Median Binance listing loses value at every horizon ([GitHub study](https://github.com/Asalio123/binance-listing-study); [4Pillars](https://research.4pillars.io/en/research/binance-listing-flash-or-fade)); pumps reverse within ~70 seconds ([Li, Shin & Wang](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3267041)) | **Moderate** | Exclude or short-bias recent listings; never chase a 15m-detected pump |
| Token unlocks | ~90% of 16,000+ unlocks show negative pressure starting ~30 days before; team cliffs worst, up to ~25% ([Keyrock](https://keyrock.com/from-locked-to-liquidity-what-16000-token-unlocks-teach-us/)) | **Moderate** (single non-peer-reviewed study) | Directional filter: suppress longs into large team/investor cliffs |
| Hacks / exploits | Governance token −27% over 24h; ~36% priced before the announcement ([NY Fed SR1153](https://newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr1153.pdf?sc_lang=en)) | **Moderate–strong** | Hard exclusion for longs on the affected token |
| Macro releases (FOMC/CPI/NFP) | Volatility and spreads jump, absorbed within ~15–30 min since 2021 ([K33](https://k33.com/research/archive/articles/all-eyes-on-wednesdays-fomc); minute-level study, [summary](https://rwatimes.substack.com/p/how-do-cryptocurrencies-price-economic)) | **Moderate** (risk), none (direction) | Risk window: no new resting entries across the release |
| Funding / carry | High carry predicts crashes ([BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm)); funding change has next-period R² ≈ 0 for single-asset price ([Presto](https://prestolabs.io/research/can-funding-rate-predict-price-change)) | **Moderate–strong** as crash/crowding risk; **none** as timing | Down-weight or tighten new longs when carry is extreme; never "short because funding is high" |
| Order-flow imbalance | Real but tiny, at 3-second horizons, IC ~0.01–0.05 ([arXiv 2602.00776](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00776v1)) | **Moderate**, seconds only | Execution-timing input for the engine, irrelevant to an hourly analyst |
| OI quadrants, L/S ratios, liquidation heatmaps, CVD divergence, Coinbase premium, max pain, ICT/SMC constructs | No forward-return tests found; heatmaps are model outputs, not orders; top-trader L/S can stay wrong for weeks ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/ecosystems/2025-11-25-btc-perp-accounts-turn-heavily-long-as-binance-long-short-ratio-surges-to-3-87-380213)); CryptoQuant calls the premium "descriptive and unstable" ([CryptoQuant](https://cryptoquant.com/insights/quicktake/6ae77a721c48840a11173491)) | **Folklore / anecdotal** | Log as features; never gate on them until measured |

The professional discretionary process does have a consistent shape. It runs regime first, then location, then trigger, and each step can veto the trade. SMB Capital's PlayBook puts "Big Picture" first among its five decision indicators and pushes traders to size up only their documented A+ plays ([TraderLion summary](https://traderlion.com/trading-books/the-playbook-summary/)). Auction market theory classifies the day by where price sits relative to value before it names a trade ([Mind Over Markets](https://www.oreilly.com/library/view/mind-over-markets/9781118659762/index.html)). The evidence for the *process itself* is indirect. No study tests top-down against non-top-down. What exists is evidence about its parts, plus a clear finding that most people who trade intraday lose. In Taiwan, day traders lost net of fees in each of 15 years and fewer than 1% outperformed consistently ([Barber et al.](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)). In Brazil, 97% of those who persisted more than 300 days lost money and there was no evidence of learning ([Chague et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)). **Inference:** the professional practices worth copying are the ones that bear on those documented losses. They are selectivity (fewer trades), pre-committed stops (against the disposition effect, which [Odean](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/-/media/documents/article/cfa-digest/1999/dig-v29-n2-469-pdf.pdf) shows costs return), and journaling by setup and regime.

The pro archetype map is rationale, not proof, but it lines up with the graded evidence. **Pullback-to-value in trend** rests on TSMOM persistence. **Range fade at extremes** rests on take-profit clustering and the modest above-random bounce rate. **Breakout-retest** rests on the finding that first breaks do not accelerate on average ([Curcio et al. via Osler 2000](https://resources.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/00v06n2/0007osle.pdf)). **Sweep-and-reclaim** rests on stops clustering beyond obvious levels ([Osler 2003](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v58y2003i5p1791-1819.html)), though no study tests the pattern itself. A claim that retests cut false breakouts from 40% to 15% has no stated method ([CoinMarketCap Academy](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/breakout-vs-fakeout-false-breakout-spot-the-difference-and-increase-accuracy)). The only cost-aware breakout study found says a coin's own autocorrelation predicts follow-through, and that hour and weekday edges vanish after 22 bps of costs ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/false-breakouts-arent-failures-theyre-the-feature)). **Inference:** each symbol's measured autocorrelation is a cheap, defensible input for choosing between continuation and fade archetypes, and it connects directly to Kaminski & Lo's condition for stops to add value.

## Derivatives data is context about crowding, not a trigger

Perp-specific data is where most crypto "analysis" lives, and where the evidence is thinnest. The best-evidenced variable is carry. BTC and ETH carry averages more than 10% a year and can reach about 60%. High carry **predicts future crashes**, because trend-chasing leveraged demand meets scarce arbitrage capital that gets margin-called in drawdowns ([BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.htm)). That is a statement about regime and crash risk over days to weeks. At the timing horizon it says nothing. A forward test on Binance BTC perps found funding changes have next-period R² ≈ 0 for price ([Presto Research](https://prestolabs.io/research/can-funding-rate-predict-price-change)). Funding's meaning is also venue-dependent. On Binance, high-funding names tended to revert, which looks like crowded retail longs. On Hyperliquid after 2024, price moved against carry, which the author hypothesises reflects informed flow ([Robot Wealth](https://edgealchemy.robotwealth.com/p/hyperliquid-carry-looks-trendy)). **Inference:** the analyst should read funding as "how crowded is this long" and lower risk on longs when it is extreme relative to the symbol's own baseline. Reading it as a fade signal, and porting thresholds across venues, are both unsupported.

Open interest should be read in coin units, not USD. Kaiko showed BTC OI at a USD all-time high while OI in BTC terms stayed below earlier peaks, which pointed to a move not driven by excess leverage ([Kaiko](https://research.kaiko.com/insights/bitcoin-rally)). The four OI-by-price quadrants are universal practitioner vocabulary, and no forward-return test was found. Liquidation data is structurally biased. Binance's forceOrder stream pushes **one liquidation per symbol per 1000ms** ([Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/websocket-market-streams/Liquidation-Order-Streams)), so it undercounts worst in the largest bursts. An arXiv study of seven Binance BTC cascades found early-warning signals were not reliable for individual crashes and inverted on one event ([Garcia Seuma](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.27070)). Resting bids in the October 2025 flash crash accumulated losing longs, while the taker side profited ([arXiv 2602.00776](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00776v1)). That is a direct warning for a limit-order engine that wants to "buy the flush". **Inference:** a liquidation print rate can serve as a cascade-in-progress flag that suspends resting long entries. "Price hunts the heatmap" and "buy after the cascade" stay untested hypotheses until the owner runs an event study. One practical constraint matters for the data pipeline: the Binance `/futures/data/*` family (OI history, L/S, taker ratio, basis) keeps only **the latest month** ([Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/market-data/rest-api/Open-Interest-Statistics)). Any baseline longer than 30 days must be recorded in-house.

Catalysts are the most useful non-price inputs, and every one of them works as a filter, not a trigger. Unlock pressure starts about 30 days before the event and stabilises about 14 days after ([Keyrock](https://keyrock.com/from-locked-to-liquidity-what-16000-token-unlocks-teach-us/)). Hack news is about one-third priced before it is public ([NY Fed SR1153](https://newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr1153.pdf?sc_lang=en)). Binance monitoring tags have coincided with tag-day drops of roughly −11% to −34% ([BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/binance-wan-token-monitoring-tag-delisting)). Exchange listings pump for one to two days and decay to deeply negative returns at three to six months ([Rockaway X](https://rockawayx.com/insights/bullish-or-bearish-what-really-happens-when-tokens-list-on-a-cex); [4Pillars](https://research.4pillars.io/en/research/binance-listing-flash-or-fade)). Daily ETF flows correlate with same-week price, but next-day correlation falls to 0.09 ([ForkLog on DWF Labs](https://forklog.com/en/dwf-labs-questions-whether-etf-flows-predict-bitcoins-future-price-moves/)), and they are published too late to matter intraday. Social sentiment has moderate academic support at extremes and short horizons, while news-media sentiment barely moves returns ([Blockworks](https://blockworks.com/news/crypto-prices-sentiment-news)). The LLM literature adds a warning on news specifically. In LiveTradeBench, news was the dominant reasoning factor and agents herded onto a headline that barely moved prices ([arXiv 2511.03628](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628v1)). **Inference:** structured catalyst flags (unlock within T−30, listing age, monitoring tag, hack, macro window) belong in the analyst's input as precomputed booleans. Free-text news should be kept out, or run as its own measured arm.

## The LLM's edge, if any, is refusal and consistency

The literature on LLM traders is unusually consistent, and it is not encouraging about prediction. Under survivorship- and look-ahead-controlled evaluation from 2000 to 2024, LLM timing strategies "consistently fail to sustain alpha", and rule-based methods beat them ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v5)). Past the model's knowledge cutoff, almost every published LLM agent fails to beat a random baseline, and TradingAgents shows a **55.68% Sharpe decay** ([Profit Mirage](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07920v1)). On memorised dates, in-sample returns can be inflated by up to 67% ([FinCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24564)). In live testing, general model rank barely correlates with trading return (Spearman **0.054** in stocks, **−0.38** on Polymarket), and reasoning models over-adjust ([LiveTradeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628v1)). In the real-money Alpha Arena crypto perp contest, more than half the field lost money in about 17 days, and overtrading was the clearest loss driver: one model reportedly made ~15 trades a day and burned over 13% of capital in fees ([Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/four-out-of-six-ai-models-suffer-losses-in-trading-tournament/); [Buildix](https://www.buildix.trade/blog/nof1-alpha-arena-lessons-ai-trading-byok-strategy-advisor-2026)). The organisers themselves said results were not a ranking ([Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/alpha-arena-nof1-ai-explained)). Agent architecture, not the model backbone, drove risk behaviour ([Agent Market Arena](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11695v1)).

The documented failure modes map straight onto design rules. Models misread chronological order and confused cash with collateral when handed raw data ([nof1 via besthub](https://www.besthub.dev/articles/can-llms-trade-crypto-profitably-inside-the-alpha-arena-competition-ace3b75ceaa5)). So the analyst should receive precomputed, labelled, ordered features, never raw candles to do arithmetic on. Verbalised confidence is overconfident, and reasoning modes worsen calibration. In one 2026 study, a stated 0.93 came true about 70% of the time ([KalshiBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16030); [arXiv 2607.08046](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.08046)). So confidence is a logged column, not a gate, until it is calibrated on forward outcomes. Outputs vary across runs even at temperature 0 ([AlphaForgeBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.18481)), and one model's accuracy fell by more than 12 points when averaged over 10 runs instead of one ([Backtrader-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.11232)). So the pipeline should sample several times and emit only setups that agree on side and level IDs. Delayed decisions degrade results in fast markets ([LiveTradeBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03628v1)). So an hourly setup needs an explicit expiry and an engine-side check that refuses if price has already left the entry zone.

**Inference (no study found):** the design under consideration, an LLM writing pre-entry limit setups that a deterministic engine executes, appears untested in the published literature. The closest positive evidence is FactFin's claim that having the LLM author *strategy rules* rather than call trades improves out-of-sample Sharpe about 1.4× over LLM-as-trader baselines. That claim is a single preprint ([Profit Mirage](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07920v1)). The defensible hypothesis has three parts. The analyst applies a validated playbook more consistently than ad hoc rules. It stands aside more often than a scanner. It adds value at the margin by combining context flags that no single gate encodes. All three are measurable, and none is established. The cheapest way to make the hypothesis falsifiable is to give the model a **level menu**. The engine supplies candidate swings, prior-day and prior-week extremes, round numbers, VWAP or value-area levels, each with an ID and timestamp. The model references IDs and the engine computes prices. That removes hallucinated levels and arithmetic error, and it makes a fixed-rule control on the identical menu possible.

### The hourly analyst checklist

The checklist below is **inference**: a synthesis of the graded evidence and the documented failure modes, not a validated procedure. Each step is a gate. A failed gate ends that symbol's evaluation with a recorded reason, and "no setup" is the expected output in most hours.

| # | Step | What the analyst reads (precomputed) | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Data integrity | Feature timestamps, bar currency, missing-field flags | If any input is stale or missing, emit nothing for that symbol and name why; never infer a missing value |
| 2 | Market risk state | BTC daily and 4h structure (MA slope, HH/HL), BTC realised vol, macro calendar window, liquidation print rate, extreme carry | Inside a FOMC/CPI/NFP window, or during an active cascade: no new resting entries. Falling BTC daily structure: no alt continuation longs |
| 3 | Symbol eligibility | Listing age, monitoring tag, hack flag, unlock within T−30 (size, recipient), 24h volume, spread, sub-$1 tick size | Recent listing, tag, hack or large team/investor cliff: exclude longs. Below the liquidity floor: exclude |
| 4 | Symbol regime | Daily and 4h trend, own autocorrelation, ATR percentile, value-area location | Classify trend / balance / expansion. "Conflicting" or "mid-range with no level" is a no-trade |
| 5 | Archetype match | Regime from step 4, plus the owner's measured performance for archetype × regime | Only an archetype allowed in this regime and with non-negative measured edge in it; never invent a new archetype |
| 6 | Location | Level menu with IDs: prior day/week H/L, HTF swings, round numbers, VWAP/value edges, distance in ATR | Entry zone must sit at a menu level; reject if price is mid-range relative to the menu |
| 7 | Invalidation | Next structural level beyond the entry, ATR | Stop beyond the structural level *and* beyond its likely stop cluster; reject if the stop is under ~1×ATR (noise) or too wide for step 8 |
| 8 | Targets and cost floor | Next opposing menu levels; fee and slippage estimate for this symbol | TP1 just before the first opposing level; the distance must clear round-trip cost by a wide margin at a plausible hit rate; leave a runner unless the archetype's measured tail says otherwise |
| 9 | Entry geometry vs drift | Current price, distance to zone, the symbol's measured drift and fill history | The zone must be reachable without chasing; refuse if price is already through it; record expected fill probability |
| 10 | Crowding overlay | Funding vs own baseline, OI change in coin units, catalyst flags | Down-weight or skip longs into extreme carry; never flip direction on it |
| 11 | Expiry and re-check | Analysis cadence | Expiry ≤ next run plus a margin; a resting order is cancelled if invalidation trades before the fill |
| 12 | Consistency and caps | K samples of the same prompt; setups already open | Emit only setups on which a majority agrees (same side and level IDs); respect per-run and per-day caps; log dispersion and stated confidence as data, not gates |

## Four measured problems, four different levers

### Entry drift becomes fill risk, not slippage

The book loses roughly 0.20% between the stamped price and the observed price (+0.14% to −0.06%), and longs drift a median 0.225%. That is larger than the whole gross edge. A pre-planned limit entry converts this cost into a different one, adverse selection, and that cost has better evidence behind it than drift does. When a passive order fills, it does so disproportionately on signals that were wrong ([Microstructure Lab](https://themicrostructurelab.substack.com/p/execution-simulation-for-hf-directional)), and limit fills show negative post-fill drift ([DeLise](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.16527)). Simulations that drop the adverse fills and assume generous fill probabilities overstate passive strategies ([arXiv 2409.12721](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.12721)). **Inference:** moving the entry ahead of price will lift the average fill price on trades that fill. Whether it lifts the *book* is an empirical question that the fill-only population cannot answer. Three recommendations follow. First, place the zone at a menu level the market must come back to, not at "current price minus a bit". Because the analyst runs hourly, the zone should anticipate a pullback rather than chase the stamp. Second, have the engine refuse any setup whose zone price has already traded through, which turns stale-hour drift into a counted refusal. Third, score every expired, unfilled setup at the outcome a taker entry at the stamp would have had, so the missed-winner cost appears beside the improved fill price. The maker fee saving (about 3 bps per side) is too small to justify the switch on its own ([inference from published fee schedules](https://vantixs.com/blog/trend-following-template-crypto-bot)).

### Tail-carried profit and limit orders are in direct conflict

This is the sharpest tension in the design. **Inference:** the top 49 trades (about +10% each) are, almost by definition, moves that ran and did not come back. A resting limit below the market is the order most likely to miss them, and most likely to fill on the trades that keep going through the level. That is the same selection mechanism the discretionary lane describes for limits at levels ([Osler 2003](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v58y2003i5p1791-1819.html) on stop clusters beyond levels). If the analyst's entries miss even a modest share of the right tail, it can turn the book negative while improving average fill price, win rate and drawdown. The recommendations are structural. Keep at least one entry mode that can catch a runner without waiting for a pullback. For continuation archetypes in expansion, that is a stop-entry through the level or a breakout-retest arm, and the evaluation should run it beside the limit arm. Treat any rule that caps the right tail as guilty until shown innocent on total PnL: a fixed tight TP, an "overextended" exclusion, a MAX-style filter. Report every arm with and without its top 1% of trades, in line with the heavy-tail findings ([Springer FMPM](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)). The analyst should also be told explicitly, in its instructions, that the objective is total PnL net of fees and that win rate is not the target. Models drift toward hit-rate-pleasing caution, and the documented bull-market timidity points the same way ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v5)).

### Break-even giveback is a stop-placement question with a known condition

About 22% of trades reach a median +2.25% favourable excursion and then close at break-even. The average TP1 is +3.6%, so the break-even shift is arming before the first target in most of these cases. Kaminski and Lo give the condition: stop rules add value only when returns are positively autocorrelated, and under a random walk they always reduce expected return ([Kaminski & Lo](https://dspace.mit.edu/bitstream/handle/1721.1/114876/Lo_When%20Do%20Stop-Loss.pdf)). A break-even stop is the tightest stop there is. Practitioner doctrine says to hold for "the real move" ([TradeThatSwing on The PlayBook](https://tradethatswing.com/key-lessons-for-day-traders-from-the-playbook-by-mike-bellafiore/)), and the disposition-effect literature shows that closing winners early costs return ([Odean](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/-/media/documents/article/cfa-digest/1999/dig-v29-n2-469-pdf.pdf)). No study tests break-even rules directly. **Inference:** two changes fit the evidence. The first anchors the protective move to structure: move the stop to just beyond the most recent confirmed higher low (for longs) once it forms, not to the entry price at a fixed excursion. The second takes a partial at the first opposing *level* when that level sits inside the typical excursion, so part of the +2.25% is banked rather than given back, while the remainder keeps a structural stop and a runner. Both are exit-shape changes, which in the owner's process are owner-sign-off items. They should be measured as paired arms over the same trades: current break-even rule, structural-trail rule, partial-at-level, and held to the original stop. That way the comparison has no population to select. The analyst can contribute only one thing here: naming in the setup the structural level that should replace break-even, so the engine has an invalidation point that is not "the entry".

### Mover-long concentration needs a liquidity and BTC gate, not a ban

Mover-pair momentum pullback longs are about 60% of the book. The evidence on that population is mixed in a way that matters. Weekly MAX looks like momentum ([Özdamar et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/fininn/v7y2021i1d10.1186_s40854-021-00291-9.html)). Hourly MAX reverses: a 1 SD hourly MAX predicts −0.043% next period ([Yadav 2025](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eme:sefpps:sef-07-2024-0461)). Momentum lives in large liquid coins, and reversal lives in small, illiquid ones ([Fičura](https://quantitative.cz/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/impact_of_size_and_volume_on_cryptocurrency_momentum_and_reversal.pdf)). The profitable window of a pump closes within about 70 seconds ([Li, Shin & Wang](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3267041)). **Inference:** a mover detected on 15m bars and held for hours sits nearer the reversal side, unless it is liquid and the market regime is up. That matches the owner's finding that trending-up states carry the book and trending-down states lose. Four recommendations follow. Gate mover longs on BTC regime (no continuation longs under a falling BTC daily structure), consistent with alts behaving as leveraged BTC ([Amberdata](https://blog.amberdata.io/performance-under-fire-2025s-risk-adjusted-reality)). Require a liquidity floor and a realistic slippage estimate before a mover qualifies, since the cost hurdle rises sharply on thin, sub-$1 names. Exclude recent listings, monitoring-tagged names and unlock-window names from the long side. Cap the mover share of open risk per hour so that one correlated BTC move cannot hit most of the book at once. One more point: with correlation near 1 in stress, several mover longs together are closer to one leveraged BTC position than to independent bets. The owner's fixed-notional sizing makes that concentration larger in money than it looks in trade counts, because the mover stops are wide.

## Evaluation must be dark, forward, paired and long

Only forward evaluation after the model's training cutoff is credible ([Profit Mirage](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.07920v1); [FinCAD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.24564)), and live benchmarks remove leakage by construction ([StockBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.02209v1)). The design that follows is **inference** from those results plus the owner's statistics. It stamps the analyst's setups dark: written, filled on paper by the same deterministic engine and fill model as live, shown to no user and sent to no exchange. Everything is pinned per row: model ID, served model, prompt schema version, input hash and level-menu version. Provider alias rotation then shows up as a population split rather than as drift.

The arms must share opportunities so the comparison is paired. The minimum set is:

| Arm | What it isolates |
|---|---|
| A. Analyst setups (limit entry at the chosen zone) | The candidate |
| B. Fixed-rule arm on the **same level menu** (e.g. nearest menu level in the HTF trend direction, stop beyond the next structural level, TP1 before the next opposing level, same expiry) | Whether the LLM adds anything beyond the menu itself |
| C. Analyst's symbol and side, taker entry at the stamp | The value (or cost) of the entry zone alone, including missed runners |
| D. Existing engine evaluator signals in the same hour and symbols | Whether the analyst beats the current production book |
| E. No-trade | The cost of trading at all, net of fees |
| F. (Exit sub-arms on filled A/B trades) current BE rule vs structural trail vs partial-at-level vs held-to-stop | The break-even question, paired per trade |

Metrics lead with **PnL % net of fees** (the owner's sizing is fixed notional, so R does not equalise trades). They report with and without the top 1% of trades. They count distinct moves, not re-stamps, so one campaign does not masquerade as many independent trades. Beside PnL, report fill rate and the taker outcome of unfilled setups, stand-aside rate, setups per day, agreement across the K samples, calibration buckets for stated confidence, and the regime split. Each regime split is shown against arm B in the same regime, because the literature documents regime-asymmetric LLM failure ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v5)).

Sample size is the constraint nobody likes. **Inference (owner's figures):** with a per-trade σ near 2.8%, detecting a +0.15%/trade improvement at 95% confidence and 80% power needs n ≈ (2.8 × 2.8 / 0.15)² ≈ **2,700 independent trades** for an unpaired comparison. A paired test (A vs B on the same opportunities) needs fewer, by a factor set by the outcome correlation between arms, which the owner should measure in the first month. The fat tail pushes the requirement the other way: when 1% of trades carries 80% of profit, a few dozen tail events decide the verdict, and the right unit of power is "how many tail events has each arm seen". At five setups a day, 2,700 trades is about 18 months, so the design should either run many symbols per hour or accept that a verdict on the *entry* question (arms A vs C, which resolves faster because fill-vs-taker differences are large) arrives long before a verdict on overall edge. Four or fewer weeks, the scale of every published live LLM contest, is noise at this effect size. Write down the refutation condition before the dark run starts. For example: if arm A does not beat arm B net of fees after N paired trades, with the CI excluding zero, the LLM adds nothing beyond the menu and the fixed rule ships instead.

## Conclusion

Two things in the owner's numbers usually get read as signal quality: the 30–34% win rate and the edge that has gone flat since October. They are better read as costs, tails and execution. The book is a tail strategy carrying a fee load, and the profit sits in a few dozen trades that a careless limit-order design is built to miss. The real opportunity for an hourly analyst is not insight. It is restraint applied consistently. It can trade fewer symbols, at levels with a structural reason, only in regimes where the archetype has paid, with entry zones that do not chase and exits that do not cap the runner. It needs no language model to do the arithmetic. It does need one to combine a dozen context flags and say no, hour after hour, without drifting.

That framing also says what would disprove the project. If a fixed rule on the same level menu matches the analyst net of fees, the model has added nothing, and the right move is to ship the rule. The menu, the catalyst flags, the BTC gate and the missed-fill accounting are worth building whichever way that test comes out. The test itself takes many months, and the owner should commit to that timescale before committing to the analyst.
